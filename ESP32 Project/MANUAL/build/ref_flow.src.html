Safety and emergencies

The customer side of every machine is safe to use. The dangers are behind the panels: 220 V mains, UV-C light, a hot heater and flammable alcohol. Read this page before you open anything.

In an emergency

  1. Stop the run. Touch CANCEL, then CONFIRM. The UV-C, the mist and the heater switch off. Box 2 and Box 3 then cool down for a while (Box 2 runs the fan, Box 3 lets everything rest). The coins are not refunded.
  2. If a door is forced open during a run, the machine shows SAFETY PAUSE and switches the UV-C, the mist and the heater off at once. It carries on when the door is shut again with the helmet inside.
  3. Fire, smoke, burning smell, sparks or a person in danger: switch the machine off at the wall socket or the main breaker, step back, and call for help. Do not use water on anything electrical.
  4. A helmet is stuck: see the next block. Never force a door while the machine has power.
The locks are power-to-unlock. With the power off, every door stays locked. So after switching off in an emergency, a helmet inside stays inside until power is back. confirm Fit a mechanical key release to each enclosure door if you want a helmet to be freeable without power.

A helmet is stuck inside

  1. Check that the machine has power. After a power cut, wait until the Control Center Home page shows the boards ONLINE again.
  2. Control Center, the box page, Maintenance, tap ENCLOSURE LOCK. The door unlocks for up to 60 seconds, then rests for 5 seconds. Tap Maintenance again when you are done.
  3. If the brain of the box stops answering for 15 seconds while a helmet is behind a closed door, the terminal board lets the enclosure lock go by itself.
  4. Still locked? Follow the decision tree The door will not open in this reference.

First aid

  • Electric shock: switch the power off first, then help. Do not touch the person while they are still in contact with a live part.
  • UV-C in the eyes or on the skin: move away from the light, and see a doctor if the eyes hurt or the skin is red.
  • Alcohol or scented liquid in the eyes: rinse with clean water for 15 minutes and get medical advice.
  • Burn from the heater: cool it under cool running water and cover it with a clean cloth.

Using the machine

  • confirm Motorcycle helmets only. Take out gloves, phones, cameras and speakers first.
  • confirm Children use the machine with an adult.
  • confirm The owner is not responsible for items left inside or for damage that comes from a helmet's own condition. Nothing is stored in the machine after a run.
  • The machine cleans and dries. It does not guarantee that every smell or every drop of water is gone.
  • Rating the helmet before and after is part of the run. The ratings are kept only as numbers.

Data and privacy

  • The machines have no camera and no microphone. They record only counts: sessions, coins, minutes, ratings and the state of each board.
  • No names, no phone numbers and no payment cards are stored. Payment is coins only.
  • The Online Server sends only totals and status to Blynk. The Blynk token is a secret: keep it out of the manual, the web page and any file that is shared.
  • The Control Center and the setup Wi-Fi use a password printed in this manual. confirm Change both before the machines are used in public, and give the admin and technician parts of this manual only to the team.

Staff and safety

  • Only trained staff refill the containers, open the panels or use Maintenance.
  • Mains work is for a qualified electrician (see Safety and emergencies).
  • Run the weekly and monthly checks in the Maintenance chapter and write the date down.
  • confirm Alcohol and scented liquid: keep the supplier's safety data sheet near the ACS. Decide where the drain water may go.

Words used in this manual, in plain language.

TermMeaning
ACSAuto Container System. The refill station: pumps and two mixer containers that top up a box's alcohol and scented containers.
Allan coin acceptorThe coin slot. It sends one pulse for each coin value it accepts.
BlynkThe online service the Online Server sends its snapshot to. The web page reads it from there.
BrainThe ESP32-S3 board of a box (S3A, S3B, S3C). It runs every step and keeps the settings. A box has one brain and several terminals.
Captive portalThe page that opens by itself when a phone joins the setup Wi-Fi of the Online Server.
Channel 1The radio channel all the boards use for ESP-NOW. The LocalServer Wi-Fi stays on it too.
Control CenterThe pages of the LocalServer (Home, Box 1, Box 2, Box 3, ACS) where settings, calibration and Maintenance are done.
Cool downThe time after heating when nothing heats and the helmet can cool. Box 2 runs the fan; Box 3 lets everything rest.
DatastreamA named value in Blynk. This project uses V0 (a text) and, if you switch them on, V1 to V4.
DC-DC moduleThe power module that turns 12 V into 5 V and 3.3 V for the boards, sensors and small loads.
Enclosure lockThe solenoid lock of the main door of a box. It is the one released for a trapped helmet.
ESP-NOWThe radio link the boards talk over. No router is needed.
ESP32 and ESP32-S3The two kinds of board. The plain ESP32 boards are terminals. The S3 boards are brains and servers.
Free retryOne free run after the final rating (2 minutes by default).
GPIOA numbered pin of an ESP32 board.
HumidifierThe ultrasonic mist maker that makes the alcohol and scented mist (5 V).
HysteresisThe gap between the WET and the DRY reading of a water sensor, so it does not flicker.
JD-VCCThe separate power input of a relay module's coils, with a jumper to the logic supply.
LocalServerThe board that makes the JollyHelmet-Local Wi-Fi and the Control Center, and counts the revenue.
Maintenance modeA page where a technician switches relays or pumps by hand. The machine shows MAINTENANCE.
MixerOne of the two containers of an ACS (alcohol, scented) where the recipe is made before it goes to a box.
MistThe alcohol or scented vapour used in cleaning. Mist method: balanced, more alcohol, more scented.
NVSThe memory inside a board where settings are saved (they survive a power cut).
Online ServerThe ESP32-S3 that listens to the machines and sends a snapshot to Blynk. It never controls anything.
OUT OF ORDERThe screen shown when a box cannot serve customers.
PhaseA part of a cleaning run: start, middle or end. Each relay is on or off per part.
Power-to-unlockA lock that opens only while it has power. With no power the door stays locked.
RatingThe 1 to 10 score the customer gives the helmet before and after: odor (Box 1 and cleaning in Box 3) or dryness (Box 2 and drying).
RelayAn electrically operated switch. The boards use relays to switch locks, pumps, mist makers, lamps and the heater.
SAFETY PAUSEThe screen shown when a door opens during a run. The loads switch off until the door is closed.
Serial MonitorThe Arduino IDE window that shows what a board prints over USB (115200 baud).
SoftAPA Wi-Fi network made by a board itself (JollyHelmet-Local, JollyHelmet-Online).
TerminalA plain ESP32 board that holds a box's sensors, relays and display. It follows its brain.
TFTThe 2.8 inch colour touch screen of a box.
Time allotThe screen where the customer types the minutes to buy.
TokenThe secret key that lets the Online Server write to your Blynk template. Keep it private.
Ultrasonic sensorThe HC-SR04. It measures the distance to the helmet or to the liquid in a container.
UV-C lampThe 220 V germicidal lamp. Never look at it when it is lit.
WatchdogA safety check: if a board stops hearing its brain for 15 seconds it switches its loads off.
