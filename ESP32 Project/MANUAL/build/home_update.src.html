Navigation bar and the Devices line

The same navigation bar sits at the top of every page: Dashboard, Settings, Box 1, Box 2, Box 3 and ACS 1 / 2. Tap a name to go there; the page you are on is highlighted. Below it the Devices line tells you how many boards answer (for example 13 of 13 online). Tap it to open the list with an ONLINE or OFFLINE badge for every board.

Daily usage and the day viewer

The chart shows how many customers came each day (Daily, the last 7 days) or each week (Weekly). Orange is the part that was cancelled. A lighter shade is the previous data collected by the team after the upgrade (see the Settings tab below).

  • Tap a bar of the Daily chart, or pick a date and tap Show the customers, to open that day customer by customer: time, box, coins, pesos, Finished or Cancelled, and minutes.
  • The two tabs Machine and JSON only tell you where each row came from: Machine is what the machines recorded, JSON is what the team added from its own records. Both are real customers.
  • Sessions without a date finished before a phone set the clock. They get their date as soon as the clock is set.

Finished and cancelled sessions

This card is the whole tally from the first day in one table: users served, coins, revenue and average time, for Finished and for Cancelled. Finished means the cycle ran to the end. Cancelled means the customer aborted in the middle; the coins that were paid still count as revenue. The line under the table says how much of the numbers came from the JSON file and how much the machines counted themselves, so nobody has to add two tables together.

A payment that is cancelled on the INSERT COIN screen is not counted as a session.

Settings tab: clock, session log, backup and previous data

The Settings tab holds what you set once and rarely touch.

  • Clock: the LocalServer has no clock of its own. A phone sets it when the Control Center is opened after a power-on, or you type the date and time. A power cut loses it.
  • Session log: every session the machines report is saved with its date and time (never a name). The bar shows how much storage is used. Entries older than a year are dropped by themselves.
  • Download log (CSV): this is the backup. Download it now and then and keep the file.
  • Restore from backup CSV: if the log is ever lost (a power cut damaged the storage, or Reset Stats was tapped by mistake), choose the CSV here and tap the button. Only an unchanged download is accepted: the last line of the file is a check that is refused if anything was edited or the file was saved again in a spreadsheet. Sessions already on the board stay, nothing is added twice, and the totals are raised to at least the log's tally.
  • Previous data (history.json): customers from before the log existed. The team builds the file with the ENTRY board (see below) or on a PC and uploads it here. The chart draws it lighter and the Finished and cancelled card adds it. It never changes the machine log, the totals or the CSV.
  • Coin value (pesos per pulse) and Reset Stats: Reset Stats empties the counters and the machine log, so download the CSV first. The history file stays.
The check line of the CSV stops mistakes and casual edits. It is not a lock. A correction to the data does not go into the machine log: it belongs in the JSON file, which is labelled as the team's own records.

Warnings and crash reports

Two red notes can appear on top of the Dashboard. The first says the board found damage in its memory. The second says the log storage was damaged and was re-created empty at the last start: open Settings, restore your last backup CSV, and send the history file again from the ENTRY board if you use it.

If the unit ever restarts by itself you do not need a laptop. Open Settings and look at This board. The last restarts are kept in the board's own flash (a power cut does not erase them). Tap Crash reports kept on this board, take a screenshot and send it to the developer. Delete these reports clears the list.

ENTRY board (spare board for past customers)

The ENTRY board is a spare ESP32 with a simple form. With it the team types in the customers of the days before the LocalServer kept dates, from the team's own records (survey forms, photos, videos and so on), and sends them to the LocalServer as the previous-data file. It is not one of the 15 boards of the machines and nothing in a box needs it.

  1. Power the ENTRY board and the LocalServer.
  2. On a phone, join the Wi-Fi JollyHelmet-Entry (password 12345678). The form opens by itself. If it does not, open a browser and go to http://192.168.5.1.
  3. Pick a day, then tap Add a customer on this day. Each customer is one card: time, box, coins, Finished or Cancelled, minutes.
  4. Type the coins and the page works out the minutes, or type the minutes and it works out the coins. The rates start with the LocalServer's values (Box 1 and Box 3: 15 coins = 5 minutes; Box 2: 10 coins = 3 minutes). The number the page worked out is shaded: type over it if the form says something else.
  5. Copy this customer repeats a card and sets the new time to the first time plus its minutes.
  6. Tap Push to the LocalServer. The board joins JollyHelmet-Local by itself and sends the file. The LocalServer checks every row first and keeps its old file if anything is wrong.
A push replaces the previous data on the LocalServer with what is on the form. Tap What is on the LocalServer now first before you add more. Never type a name. If the LocalServer's Wi-Fi name or password is changed in its firmware, change LS_SSID and LS_PASS at the top of ESP32_ENTRY.ino too.

OPEN A / OPEN B beep

While a box waits at OPEN A or OPEN B for the door to be opened, it beeps again and again (beep, wait, beep, wait) until the door opens. You set the length of the beep and of the wait on each box page, in the section OPEN A / OPEN B beep.

  • Beep for 20 to 1000 ms, then wait 0 to 10000 ms. A wait of 0 gives one beep only.
  • Tap Send. The box keeps the values in its own memory. The default is a beep of 100 ms and a wait of 500 ms.

The 2-second safety and the drain pump warning

So that a forgotten tap cannot leave a lamp, a humidifier, the heater or a lock running, every relay you switch on in Maintenance switches itself off again after 2 seconds. The box does this by itself, so it also works if the phone or the page disconnects.

  • Bypass Safety 2 Seconds Relay: tick it when a relay must stay on, for example to watch a humidifier make mist. The safety is then off for every relay until you untick the box or leave Maintenance. The heater still turns itself off after 5 minutes (Box 2 and Box 3) and the enclosure lock rests after 60 seconds.
  • Drain pumps and coin power are not part of the 2-second safety (Box 1 and Box 3). They stay on until you switch them off.
  • Drain pump warning: tapping a drain pump first shows a WARNING window ("You are now toggling Drain Pump 1 ON"). Tap Yes, turn it ON only when the drain tube is connected and the container can take the liquid. No, cancel (or Esc, or a tap outside the window) does nothing, and No is the default.
Check the drain tube and the container before you tap Yes. A drain pump that is left on keeps pumping until you switch it off.

Optional customer names (Blynk V5)

A customer can type a nickname (12 characters at most) on the card of the machine that is running his or her cycle. Everybody who opens the customer page sees it ("Maria's helmet"), the customer's own phone says the helmet is done at the end, and the name clears by itself when the cycle ends. It is optional: no name, nothing shown.

  • One extra datastream, once: in the Blynk Console, open the template of the public device, Datastreams, New, Virtual Pin V5, name names, data type String. Without it the page works as before and a customer who tries to add a name is told that names are not switched on yet.
  • A name can be added only after the coins are in, so a payment that is cancelled never leaves a name behind. The phone that typed it can edit or remove it; other phones can only see it.
  • The name is shown only while the box is looking after a helmet (OPEN A to ABORT) and only for that cycle. Flash the Online Server firmware of 2026-10-14 or later so it adds each box's session counter to the public value.
  • Whatever sits in V5 is treated as untrusted text: the page strips < and > and control characters and shows it only as plain text. The page tells the customer to use a nickname, because the name is public to everybody who opens the page.
  • Cost: a few Blynk messages for each customer who adds a name, nothing for the others. In js/config.js, names: false hides the feature and namesPin chooses the datastream.
